## Why you need it

Almost all the exercises in the course happen **right on this site**: quizzes, a simulated terminal, files to write. The site checks them and tells you what works and what is missing.

To become truly independent, though, at some point you need to use **real** Docker. That is why some missions are **labs**: exercises done on a real computer with Docker installed. This lesson gets you ready, so everything is set up when the time comes.

<Callout tipo="info">
You don't need to do this right away. The first lab comes in World 1. You can read this lesson now and create the account later.
</Callout>

## A computer that is not yours: the cloud

Installing Docker on your own computer is not always possible: you need a fairly recent computer, permission to install programs and some configuration. To avoid these obstacles we will use a computer that is **somewhere else** and that you control from your browser.

We say that computer is in the <Termine id="cloud">cloud</Termine>: a real computer, sitting in a company's data center, that you can use over the internet without having it physically in front of you.

<Analogia>
It is like a professional kitchen you can book by the hour: you don't have to buy ovens and tools, you walk in, cook, and when you are done you leave it. They have already put the pans in place.
</Analogia>

## GitHub and Codespaces

<Termine id="github">GitHub</Termine> is a website where people who develop software keep and share their projects. We will use it a lot in World 4, where we will look at it properly. For now we need it for one specific reason: it offers **Codespaces**.

<Termine id="codespaces">GitHub Codespaces</Termine> is a service that gives you, in a few seconds, a ready-made computer in the cloud. You use it from a browser tab, like a normal website. For every lab the course will give you an **"Open in Codespaces"** button that prepares everything you need, Docker included.

## How much it costs

A personal GitHub account is free. Codespaces includes a **free monthly usage quota**: when this lesson was checked, for free accounts, 120 hours of compute and 15 GB of storage per month. Hours are used up faster if you choose more powerful machines: the smallest one is enough for the course.

Without a payment method on file, when the quota runs out Codespaces simply stops until the next month: **there are no surprise charges**.

<Callout tipo="attenzione">
The quota numbers may change. Before starting the labs, check the official Codespaces billing page linked at the end of this lesson.
</Callout>

## Creating your GitHub account

1. Go to **github.com** and choose **Sign up**. You can also sign up with a Google or Apple account.
2. Follow the on-screen instructions to create a free personal account.
3. **Verify your email address**: without it some features are not available.
4. Turn on **two-factor authentication**: besides your password, signing in requires a code from your phone. GitHub strongly recommends it, and it protects your account.

<Callout tipo="consiglio">
Use a personal email address, not one from a company or school: GitHub does not accept, for a personal account, an address already used in an account managed by an organization.
</Callout>

## In short

- Most exercises happen on the site; **labs** use real Docker.
- The **cloud** is a computer somewhere else that you use over the internet.
- **GitHub** is a site to keep and share projects; **Codespaces** gives you a ready-made computer in the cloud, inside your browser.
- The account is free and Codespaces has a free monthly quota: without a payment method there are no charges.
